## Description (paste as is)

The Moon is leaving us 🌕 Every year it drifts about 3.8 cm farther from Earth. Rewind four billion years, and it was so close it filled the sky. One day, it will be too small to cover the Sun, and total solar eclipses will end.

Facts:
• The Moon recedes about 3.8 cm a year, measured with laser reflectors left by Apollo astronauts
• The young Moon formed only about 20,000 km away (today: 384,400 km)
• In about 600 million years, if today's rate holds, the Moon will be too small for a total eclipse

What happens to the very last eclipse? Watch the other Short: The Last Solar Eclipse Ever.

Sources: NASA, Eos, Scientific American (Phil Plait), Lantink et al., PNAS (2022).
