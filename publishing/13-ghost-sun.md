## Description (paste as is)

```
What would actually happen if the Sun disappeared? It's twelve o'clock, noon, and the Sun is already gone: the light on your face left it 8 minutes and 20 seconds ago. So we try an experiment. The Sun disappears for exactly 8 minutes and 20 seconds, then comes back. By the time the lights go out on Earth, the Sun is already back. Then we ask the bigger question: what if it never came back? In the end, even the air would freeze and fall out of the sky.

What do you think would survive longest? Tell us in the comments.

Sources and notes:
The Sun can't really vanish (mass doesn't just disappear); this is a thought experiment. Numbers are rounded; planet distances are averages; drawings are not to scale.
- Light and gravity both take about 8 min 20 s to reach us: Live Science; IFLScience (2026)
- Gravity and light arrived 1.7 s apart from a neutron-star collision 130 million light-years away (GW170817): LIGO-Virgo and Fermi, ApJ 848 L13 (2017)
- Longest possible total eclipse, 7 min 31 s: Jean Meeus, via Live Science
- Temperature drop of 3-4°C during the 2017 total eclipse: American Meteorological Society annual meeting, 2018
- Bees went silent during totality: Galen et al. 2018, Annals of the Entomological Society of America
- Cooling about 20°C a day; ponds, lakes and oceans freezing: George Mason University planetary scientist Michael Summers, via Live Science
- About -73°C after a year and -240°C in the end: Caltech's David Stevenson, via Popular Science
- Oxygen would last about 2 million years: The Astrobiology Primer, Astrobiology 6:735 (2006)
- Tides: NOAA Ocean Service
- Tardigrades in open space for 10 days: ESA FOTON-M3, Jönsson et al., Current Biology (2008)
- Hydrothermal vent life, discovered 1977: Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution; NOAA Ocean Explorer
- Voyager 1 one light-day from Earth (18 Nov 2026): NASA/JPL
```
