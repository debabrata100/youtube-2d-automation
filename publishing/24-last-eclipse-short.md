## Description (paste as is)

```
One day, the Sun will go dark for the very last time 🌑 Total solar eclipses only work because the Sun is about 400 times wider than the Moon and about 400 times farther away: a perfect fit, by pure luck.

But the Moon drifts about 3.8 cm farther from Earth every year. In about 600 million years, if today's rate holds, it will be too small to cover the Sun. After that, only rings of fire.

Facts:
• Sun about 400× wider than the Moon, and about 400× farther away
• Moon recedes about 3.8 cm a year (Apollo laser ranging)
• Last total solar eclipse in about 600 million years (NASA's Noah Petro)

The Moon used to fill the whole sky. Watch the other Short: The Moon Is Leaving Us.

Sources: NASA, Eos.
```
