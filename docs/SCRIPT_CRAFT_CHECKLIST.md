# Script craft checklist: how to connect facts into a story

Use this on every long-episode script from the first draft (it applies to the M1 Moon script first). It comes from comparing
our Ep 5 script with Astrum's "What Voyager 1 Found Beyond the Edge of Our Solar System" (1.35M views in 43 hours,
18–20 Nov 2026; full transcript read 20 Nov). Tick each item, or write why it doesn't apply.

## Structure
- [ ] **One main character carries every fact.** Each fact is a step in that character's journey (Voyager, Earth, the Moon), not a list item.
- [ ] **Cold open = news or a surprise + a precise number + stakes + the question**, then the brand sting, then a one-line preview of what's coming.
- [ ] **Every chapter ends with a bridge sentence** that creates anticipation ("But this wasn't the edge just yet." / "And just when we thought it couldn't get any weirder…"). No chapter starts cold.

## Linking facts
- [ ] **Then and now:** at least 3 facts are linked to what came later or what's happening today ("measured in 1979 → it has shrunk 50% since"; "found in 1977 → today NASA looks for the same under Europa's ice").
- [ ] **Cause and consequence chains:** a choice or discovery that led to the next one (choosing Titan → Voyager 2 went on to Neptune).
- [ ] **Callbacks:** at least 2 moments refer back to something earlier in the video (and pay off the cold open's promise).

## Making it memorable
- [ ] **Real stakes where the topic allows:** a near-loss, a risk, a deadline, a measured decline (4 W a year).
- [ ] **Named real people in the narration** (not only on screen): who found it, when, and one short quote where it helps.
- [ ] **Every big number gets an everyday comparison** (a gaming PC, a car key fob, London to New York in 3 minutes).
- [ ] **Questions asked, then answered:** Arka asks the viewer's question; sometimes the narrator sets up a wrong guess ("You might imagine…, but you would be wrong").

## Our own strengths (keep them)
- [ ] Arka's humor at least once per chapter; new jokes only (repetition rules).
- [ ] Ending: a personal, warm line, then Arka's next question as an honest open loop (the teased script must be drafted).
- [ ] 10–12 minutes at our stage; longer only when the story genuinely needs it.

## Free fixes after narration
When the narration is already approved and paid for, add missing links as **on-screen notes** (then-and-now, comparisons, names)
and in the description and pinned comment, rather than re-recording.

## Shorts: the first second (from the Voyager Shorts, 21 Nov 2026)
- [ ] **Open on the shock or the stakes, not a date or a setup.** "NASA lost contact with Voyager 1" beats "On October eighteenth, NASA called Voyager 1". Put dates and context in the second line. (V3 opened on a date: 61% swiped away in its first two hours; V1 opened on the hook, "Right now, a message from Earth needs a full day...": 46% stayed, our best.)
- [ ] **The first frame shows the subject and the problem at once** (the probe, a cross, a number), so the line and the picture land together in the first second.

## Grown-up audience (positioning, 21 Nov 2026)
Our viewer is a curious 16–35-year-old, not a child. A cartoon style is fine; a children's tone is not.
- [ ] **Stakes and topics for adults:** real science, real numbers and sources, consequences that matter to grown-ups (energy, work, cities, extinction), told calmly. Dark themes stay non-graphic.
- [ ] **Arka's humour is dry or ironic, never babyish:** adult references (Mondays, coffee, deadlines, taxes, a tan), no baby talk, no "yay!" or silly noises as the joke.
- [ ] **Normal adult vocabulary;** never "kids", "boys and girls", "let's learn", or explaining as if to a child. Explain clearly, don't talk down.
- [ ] **No children's songs, nursery rhymes or sing-along** moments; no toy-like sound effects (boings, squeaks) as gags.
- [ ] **Titles, descriptions and tags written for adults** and honest (no fear-mongering, no "for kids"); audience setting "No, it's not made for kids".
