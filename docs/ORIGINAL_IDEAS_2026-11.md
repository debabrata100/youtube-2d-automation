# Original-question ideas — November 2026

Researched 24 Nov 2026. The goal is ideas that **no one has filmed yet**, so we are not chasing a topic someone else already owns. Every number below was computed (python, CoolProp IAPWS-95 for water) from the sourced inputs listed. The coverage check uses web search only, so "not found" means "not found in search", not "proven absent".

The four idea types:

1. **Original computed number**: we work out an answer nobody has published.
2. **News in its first 72 hours**: we ship before the big channels.
3. **India-first**: a familiar idea told with Indian places and objects.
4. **Arka's experiment**: Arka tries something everyday somewhere impossible.

---

## 1. Arka makes chai across the Solar System  *(Arka's experiment)*

**Hook:** "Your chai would boil hotter on Titan than in Kolkata."

| Place | Air pressure | Water boils at |
|---|---|---|
| Kolkata (sea level) | 101.3 kPa | 100.0 °C |
| Darjeeling (2,045 m, estimate) | ~79.5 kPa | ~93 °C |
| Everest summit | 33.7 kPa | 71.8 °C |
| **Titan** | 146.7 kPa | **110.7 °C** |
| Mars, average | 0.61 kPa | 0 °C, so it freezes and boils at once |
| Mars, Hellas basin floor | ~1.16 kPa | 9.1 °C |
| Venus | 9.2 MPa | 305 °C, but the ground is ~465 °C, so no liquid |

**Story beats:**
- Kolkata: perfect.
- Everest: weak, pale tea.
- Mars: the kettle freezes and boils at the same time.
- Venus: the water never even stays liquid.
- Titan, the twist: a hotter boil than home, in −179 °C air.

**Already covered?**
- Two Leicester student papers from 2013 did the physics: "Tea Time in the Solar System" and "The Perfect Cuppa". The second concludes Titan is the only place for an Earth-like cup.
- No YouTube Short with this chai/Arka framing turned up. Mars "your blood boils" videos are common, but they cover a different angle.

**Why it can trend:** chai is the most relatable object in India, and the twist runs against intuition (a frozen moon makes a better boil).

**Effort:** new props (kettle, cup, Titan surface). Narration is about 450–550 characters, so about 500 ElevenLabs credits.

## 2. If the Sun were a cricket ball at Eden Gardens  *(India-first)*

Scale: Sun = 7.2 cm cricket ball, which makes the model 1 : 19.3 billion.

| Object | Model size | Distance from the ball |
|---|---|---|
| Earth | 0.66 mm (a grain of sand) | 7.7 m, not even halfway down the 20.12 m pitch |
| Moon | — | 2 cm from that grain |
| Mars | — | 11.8 m |
| Jupiter | 7 mm | 40 m |
| Saturn | 6 mm | 74 m, out near the boundary rope |
| Neptune | 2.5 mm | 233 m, outside the stadium |
| Voyager 1 (one light-day) | — | 1.3 km, past the Maidan |
| **Nearest star (Proxima)** | another cricket ball | **~2,080 km away. Kanyakumari is 1,981 km from Eden Gardens** |

**Hook:** "Put the Sun on the pitch at Eden Gardens. The nearest star is a second cricket ball… in Kanyakumari."

**Already covered?**
- The idea itself is classic: football, basketball and peppercorn versions exist (Astrum, Planetary Society, NASA, Dawkins). Mark Rober did a drone-and-field version.
- No cricket-ball or Indian-ground version turned up.

**Why it can trend:** cricket plus scale is instantly shareable in India, and anyone can fact-check it with a map.

**Before scripting:** check Eden's actual boundary length, so the Saturn line is honest ("near the boundary rope").

## 3. The ISS goes from Kolkata to Delhi in 3 minutes  *(original computed number)*

The ISS flies at about 7.66 km/s at ~420 km. The point under it moves across the ground at **7.19 km/s**.

| Trip at that speed | Great-circle distance | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Kolkata → Darjeeling | 497 km | 69 s |
| Kolkata → Delhi | 1,304 km | **3 min** |
| Kolkata → Mumbai | 1,655 km | 3 min 50 s |
| All of India, north to south | 3,214 km | 7.5 min |

**Hook:** "A Rajdhani needs 17 hours. The space station needs 3 minutes."

**Already covered?**
- Lots of news on ISS passes over India (the Shubhanshu Shukla sightings in 2025) and on "crosses your sky in 4–6 min".
- No city-to-city framing turned up.

**Caveat:** the real ground track crosses India diagonally, not along these lines. Script it as "at that speed".

**Weakness:** this is the least surprising idea. It works better as a 6–8 s loop Short than as a full Short.

## 4. Hera's first close-up of the asteroid humans moved  *(news, first 72 h)*

- ESA's Hera arrived at Didymos–Dimorphos in November 2026.
- The first detailed Dimorphos images are being released on **Wed 25 Nov**, livestream from 19:45 CET (00:15 IST on 26 Nov).
- It's the first close look since NASA's DART hit it in 2022.

**Hook:** "In 2022 we punched an asteroid. Tonight we finally see the bruise."

**Timing problem:** to count as news, this has to go out by about 27–28 Nov, which collides with the loop test (R1 and G1 go public on 27 Nov). It would also need new narration and props within 48 hours.

**Recommendation:** skip this one. Use the daily news check to catch the next story early enough to plan for it.

## 5. Vyommitra: India's first "astronaut" is a robot  *(news ready to fire, plus India-first)*

- Gaganyaan G1 will carry the half-humanoid robot Vyommitra.
- Its launch has been promised for Dec 2024, Dec 2025, and now Dec 2026.
- Reports from 20 Nov say it may slip to February 2027, and ISRO has confirmed neither date.

**Plan:**
- Build the Short now: who Vyommitra is, what she will test, and why a robot flies first.
- Hold it, then publish within hours of ISRO announcing the date.

This gives us a news story in its first 72 hours without the rush.

**Already covered?**
- Indian news channels will cover the launch.
- An animated "why the robot goes first" explainer ready on launch day is rare.

**Tone:** stay neutral about the delays. Just state the dates.
