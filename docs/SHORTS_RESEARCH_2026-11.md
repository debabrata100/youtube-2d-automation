the twist (a surprise people can't predict from the title), the first second, and drawing every real object as itself.

## Ranked plan — 6 Shorts

### 1. "You've never left Earth's atmosphere. Neither has anyone."
- **First second:** Arka floats past the Moon in a spacesuit, proud. A thin blue haze is still around him. Text: "STILL INSIDE".
- **Twist:** Earth's outermost layer, a thin hydrogen cloud called the geocorona, reaches about 630,000 km. That is 50 Earths across and almost twice as far as the Moon. So the Moon moves through Earth's atmosphere. Artemis II set the human distance record in April 2026 at 406,771 km, so even that crew never left it.
- **Honesty line (say it):** out there it is thinner than any vacuum we can make in a lab. Scientists measure it by the faint ultraviolet glow of hydrogen.
- **Beats (~65 s):** Arka at the Moon → zoom out to Earth with a 100 km line ("where space 'starts'") → haze grows to the Moon's orbit → beyond it to 630,000 km → Apollo 16's 1972 ultraviolet photo of the glow, taken from the Moon (drawn as a photo plate) → SOHO's SWAN data from 1996–98, re-checked in 2019 → Orion capsule on its record loop, still inside the haze → end: "Every astronaut ever: still technically home."
- **Sources:** [ESA 2019](https://www.esa.int/Our_Activities/Space_Science/Earth_s_atmosphere_stretches_out_to_the_Moon_and_beyond); [SOHO hotshot](https://soho.nascom.nasa.gov/hotshots/2019_02_20); [NASA Artemis II return](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-welcomes-record-setting-artemis-ii-moonfarers-back-to-earth/); [AP via OPB, 406,771 km](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/04/10/artemis-ii-astronauts-return-from-moon-with-a-splashdown-to-close-out-a-record-breaking-lunar-voyage).
- **Why people stay:** the title contradicts something every viewer is sure of, and the Artemis II record (2026) makes it new.

### 2. "Earth is rusting the Moon" (part 2 of #1)
- **First second:** a close-up of red-brown spots on the grey Moon. Arka scratches one, and rust flakes off.
- **Twist:** Chandrayaan-1's Moon Mineralogy Mapper (built by JPL) found hematite, which is rust, near the Moon's poles. Rust needs oxygen, and the Moon has none. The oxygen comes from Earth: our magnetic tail carries it out. For up to about 5 days every orbit, the Moon passes through this "Earth wind". During those days the tail also blocks most of the solar wind's hydrogen, which normally stops rust from forming.
- **Beats (~60 s):** grey Moon → red spots → "the Moon has no air" (crossed out) → Earth with its magnetic field stretched into a long tail → full Moon slides into the tail → oxygen drifts across → rust forms on the side facing Earth → callback: "Remember the atmosphere that reaches the Moon?"
- **Sources:** [NASA/JPL 2020](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/the-moon-is-rusting-and-researchers-want-to-know-why/); Li et al., *Science Advances* 2020; Wang et al., *ApJL* 2021 (Earth wind, up to 5 days per orbit).
- **Why people stay:** a two-part link ("Part 2") gives returning viewers and binge chains. The India angle (Chandrayaan-1 found it) helps our core audience.

### 3. "NASA is paying $843 million to crash its own space station"
- **First second:** the ISS drawn crisp. A tow truck in space with a "SpaceX" plate hooks onto it. Arka: "Wait, crash it?"
- **Twist:** the ISS is too big to burn up safely. In June 2024 NASA gave SpaceX a contract worth up to $843M for the US Deorbit Vehicle. About a year before the end, it docks, then steers the station down into Point Nemo. That is the spot in the ocean farthest from any land, and it already holds more than 260 retired spacecraft.
- **Beats (~70 s):** ISS in orbit → "football-field size" comparison drawn over a field → why burning up is not enough (pieces survive) → deorbit vehicle docks → map of the South Pacific (no borders) with Point Nemo marked → "nearest humans are often astronauts overhead" → spacecraft cemetery under the sea → planned 2030 end.
- **Check before scripting:** confirm the current retirement year and vehicle status (plans can slip).
- **Sources:** NASA news release on the US Deorbit Vehicle contract, 26 Jun 2024; NASA ISS transition plan.

### 4. "She died 11 days after NASA launched her voice"
- **First second:** a 1935 gramophone becomes a golden disc and flies off. A date card flips: "5 Sep 1977 … 16 Sep 1977".
- **Twist (new angle; the 2.3M Short only says "her voice is in space"):** Kesarbai Kerkar's 1935 recording "Jaat Kahan Ho" (raga Bhairavi, 3:30) is the only Indian track on the Voyager Golden Record. The song's title asks "Where are you going?" Voyager 1 launched on 5 September 1977, and she died on 16 September 1977. The question in her song is now about 25 billion km from Earth and still travelling.
- **Beats (~60 s):** Bombay 1935 studio → the record's selection committee (Sagan, ethnomusicologist Robert E. Brown picks her) → Voyager 2 launches 20 Aug 1977, Voyager 1 on 5 Sep → calendar to 16 Sep → Voyager leaving the Sun's bubble → closing card: "Where are you going?"
- **Do not:** play or sing the recording, or show lyrics beyond the title and its meaning. Use original music only.
- **Check before scripting:** one Wikipedia version marks her death year with "(?)". Caravan, The Print and Wikipedia's infobox give 16 Sep 1977. Confirm with one more source (Sangeet Natak Akademi or a newspaper obituary).
- **Sources:** [NASA Golden Record list](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/golden-record-contents/sounds/); [Caravan](https://caravanmagazine.in/vantage/kesarbai-becomes-malkauns); [JPL Voyager 1 launch](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia21747-voyager-1-launch/).

### 5. "In 2029, one minute may have only 59 seconds"
- **First second:** a digital clock goes 23:59:58 → 00:00:00, skipping :59. Arka stares at it.
- **Twist:** Earth is spinning faster. On 5 July 2024 the day was 1.66 ms short, the shortest since atomic clocks began, and summer 2025 nearly matched it. Since 1972 we have added 27 leap seconds and never removed one. A 2024 study in *Nature* (Agnew) says a first negative leap second may be needed around 2029. Melting polar ice has slowed Earth enough to push that back from about 2026.
- **Beats (~65 s):** spinning Earth with a stopwatch → bar of 27 added seconds → recent record days → liquid core and Moon tides as causes (keep it simple) → ice melting at the poles and spreading toward the equator, slowing the spin ("like a skater opening her arms") → servers and clocks never tested with a skipped second → ends on "may" — scientists aren't sure.
- **Sources:** [National Geographic, Jul 2025](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/shortest-day-earth-rotation); [Space.com](https://www.space.com/astronomy/earth/earth-will-spin-faster-on-july-22-to-create-2nd-shortest-day-in-history); Agnew, *Nature* 2024.

### 6. "Earth's second moon might be a piece of our Moon — and China just reached it"
- **First second:** Earth with two moons, one tiny and tumbling. Arka double-takes.
- **Twist:** Kamo'oalewa, found in 2016 from Hawaii, travels alongside Earth as a quasi-moon. Tianwen-2's 2026 images suggest it is only about 27 m across (some reports say 16 m), and it spins about every 30 minutes. Its reddish, weathered spectrum looks like Moon rock, though one scientist who saw the first image thought it looked more like an ordinary asteroid. China's Tianwen-2 reached it in 2026 and photographed it. It aims to collect 20–100 mg, leave in April 2027, and land the sample in the Gobi desert in late 2027, so the answer is coming.
- **Beats (~65 s):** two moons → its odd orbit drawn as a loop around Earth from our point of view → a Giordano Bruno crater impact throwing out debris → Tianwen-2 arrives (drawn as the real spacecraft shape) → first image → sample capsule falling to the desert → "In 2027 we find out."
- **Check before scripting:** older papers estimated 40–100 m. Use the 2026 imaging numbers, and say plainly that the Moon origin is still open. That open question is the hook.
- **Sources:** Tianwen-2 mission reports (CNSA); Sharkey et al. 2021; Jiao et al., *Nature Astronomy* 2024.

## Reserve topics (verified, lower priority)

- **19-hour day for a billion years.** For roughly 2 to 1 billion years ago, the length of a day stayed near 19 hours. The Moon's tide and the Sun's atmospheric tide balanced each other. Sources: Mitchell & Kirscher, *Nature Geoscience* 2023; University of Toronto study in *Science Advances*.
- **Mars isn't red because of rust like we thought.** The red comes from ferrihydrite, a mineral that forms in cool water. Source: Valantinas et al., *Nature Communications*, Feb 2025.
- **Mars craters named after UP and Bihar towns.** The IAU approved Lal, Mursan and Hilsa on 5 Jun 2024. Mursan is the PRL director's birthplace. This works only for an Indian audience, and competition has not been checked yet.
- **The Moon is shrinking.** About 50 m over hundreds of millions of years. A shallow moonquake of magnitude 5 in 1973 is linked to faults near the planned Artemis landing sites. Source: Watters et al., *PSJ*, Jan 2024.
- **Vikram's 40 cm hop.** On 3 Sep 2023, ISRO tested taking off again: Vikram rose about 40 cm and landed 30–40 cm away.

## Rules for every Short in this set

- Draw every named object as itself (ISS, Orion, Tianwen-2, the gramophone, the Point Nemo map with no borders).
- The twist lands within 3 seconds; the title only hints at it.
- Show units and comparisons as pictures, not just numbers.
- End on a line that loops back to the first frame, so replays count.
- Cost: all free (local render, existing voice workflow). Any paid narration needs approval first, as always.
