# Voyager 1 one-light-day Short: script and fact check

Timely Short for the week Voyager 1 crosses one light-day from Earth (NASA's projection: 18 Nov 2026, 10:16:07 UTC = 3:46 PM IST). Format: 9:16, about 40 s, narrator plus Arka (who is riding Voyager), linked to Ep 3 as its related video. The script lives in `scripts/authoring/plan-voyager-short.py`.

## Script (draft 2: more information, channel signature at the end)

| Shot | Speaker | Line |
|---|---|---|
| v1 (hook) | Narrator | Right now, a message from Earth needs a full day to reach Voyager 1. |
| v2 | Narrator | It's the first thing we've ever built that's one light-day away. Twenty-six billion kilometers. |
| v3 | Arka / Narrator | Hi, Earth! / Say hi back, and Arka hears it tomorrow. |

| v6 | Narrator | Its computers: sixty-nine kilobytes. Less than one photo on your phone. |
| v7 | Narrator / Arka / Narrator | Its radio: about as strong as a fridge light bulb. / And we still hear it? / Barely. But yes. |
| v8 | Narrator | It carries a golden record: sounds and pictures of Earth, for anyone who finds it. |
| v9 | Narrator | In 1990, it looked back and took this. That tiny dot is every person who ever lived. (NASA photo PIA23645) |
| v10 | Narrator | Then its cameras went dark, and it kept going. |
| v11 | Narrator / Arka | A tiny light in the deep dark, still talking to us. / Call you tomorrow! (end card: "Gahanarka: light in the unfathomable") |

Extra facts for draft 2: Jupiter flyby March 1979, Saturn November 1980; at one light-day Voyager 1 is about 5.6 times Neptune's distance (Neptune is about 4.5 billion km from the Sun); each Voyager carries a 12-inch gold-plated record with sounds and images of Earth (NASA, PIA14113); the Pale Blue Dot was taken 14 Feb 1990 from about 6 billion km (NASA, PIA23645).

## Fact check (sources consulted 17 Nov 2026)

| Claim | Basis |
|---|---|
| One light-day on 18 Nov 2026; first human-made object that far | NASA projection, reported by EarthSky, IFLScience and others: 18 Nov 2026, 2:16:07 a.m. PST; one light-day = 25.9 billion km. |
| A message takes a whole day; a reply two days | At one light-day, a radio signal needs 24 hours one way (NASA's Suzy Dodd via CNN). "Arka hears it tomorrow" = one way. |
| Flying since 1977 | Launched 5 Sep 1977. |
| Seventeen kilometers every second | About 17 km/s relative to the Sun (about 61,000 km/h, 38,000 mph). |
| About sixty-nine kilobytes; less than one phone photo | The three computer systems hold a combined 69.63 KB (figure cited by NASA engineers); one phone photo is several MB. |
| Radio as strong as a fridge light bulb | The transmitter runs at about 22-23 W, commonly compared to a refrigerator bulb. |
| We can barely hear it | The signal arriving at the Deep Space Network's 70 m dishes is around 10^-18 W. |
| 1990: turned around, one last photo, pale blue dot | The Pale Blue Dot (14 Feb 1990) was part of the Solar System "family portrait"; the cameras were switched off afterwards. |
| Cameras switched off for good | Powered off permanently after 1990; the imaging software was later deleted to save memory. |
| Still talking to us | Still returning data in 2026; power about 220-230 W and falling about 4 W a year; expected to fall silent in the early-to-mid 2030s. |

## Honesty notes

- Arka "riding Voyager" is a playful framing; no one is aboard.
- "This week" keeps the line true if the Short goes out between 18 and 22 Nov.
- Arka's line reaching Earth "tomorrow" assumes one-way light time at the milestone.
