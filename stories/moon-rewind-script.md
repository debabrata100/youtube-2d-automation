# M1 "Could the Moon Ever Come Back?": script draft 1

Long episode, 16:9, target **10–12 minutes**. Narrator: **Gahanarka R1**. Arka: **Arka E3** (warm, curious, grown-up; chosen by Debabrata in a blind test on 22 Nov 2026, replacing D2).

**Title (chosen by Debabrata, 21 Nov 2026):** **Could the Moon Ever Come Back?** (30 characters). A "Could…" shape after two "If/What if" titles.
**Thumbnail idea (Debabrata, 21 Nov):** the second title option becomes the thumbnail promise: a giant Moon over the young Earth, Arka tiny below it, text **"IT USED TO BE THIS CLOSE"**.

**Pays off Ep 5's ending:** Arka asked "Why is it leaving? Could it ever come back?" This episode answers both, and ends with an honest "no", then the moon that *is* coming back (Phobos).

**Angle (crowded-topic rule, 21 Nov 2026):** the plain "the Moon is moving away" explainer is everywhere (StarTalk, SciShow and many small channels), and Ep 4 already gave the basics (3.8 cm a year, the mirrors, the tidal brake, the giant-impact birth). So this is not that explainer. It's a **thought experiment: rewind the Moon**, stopping only where real evidence exists, each stop a real person and a real object:
1. **1969, the mirror** (Buzz Aldrin, the laser): how we know, and why today's rate can't simply be rewound (the 1.5-billion-year paradox).
2. **136 BC, the eclipse in the wrong place** (a Babylonian clay tablet; Richard Stephenson's 40 years of records).
3. **380 million years ago, the coral calendar** (John Wells, 1963: about 400 days a year).
4. **2.46 billion years ago, the rusty cliff** (Margriet Lantink, 2022: 17-hour days), then **the billion-year stall** at 19 hours (Mitchell & Kirscher, 2023).
5. **4.5 billion years ago, the giant in the sky** (the payoff of the thumbnail).
6. **The answer:** in theory it *would* come back, after 50 billion years, as rubble; in reality the Sun runs out of time first.
7. **Twist:** Phobos *is* coming back, and it's the same rule seen from the other side.

**Structure device (repetition rule 13):** a **rewind counter** that stays on screen through the whole episode: YEAR · MOON DISTANCE · LENGTH OF DAY. Each chapter is one stop on the counter, going backwards. Ep 4 ran forwards in elapsed time, Ep 5 by temperature; this one rewinds through time.

**Arka from this episode on (grown-up positioning, 21 Nov 2026):** dry, curious, a colleague rather than a kid sidekick. Real questions and deadpan jokes about adult life (to-do lists, Mondays, lunch, replies to messages); no squeals, no "whoa!".

### Chapter 1: 1969 · The mirror on the Moon

| # | Speaker | Line |
|---|---|---|
| r9 | Narrator | Stop one: July 1969. Buzz Aldrin sets down a small panel on the Moon: a hundred glass prisms in a frame. |
| r10 | Narrator | Each prism sends light straight back where it came from. So scientists on Earth fire a laser at it, and wait. |
| r11 | Narrator | About two and a half seconds later, the light comes home. Only a few particles of light from each pulse make it back. But that's enough. |

| r13 | Narrator | Time that round trip, and you know the distance to about a millimeter. Observatories have done it for over fifty years, and every year the answer creeps up by three point eight centimeters. |

| r15 | Narrator | The bulge ahead tugs the Moon forward, lifting it into a higher orbit. And the Moon pulls back on the bulge, braking Earth's spin. |
| r16 | Narrator | So the Moon drifts out, and our days get longer, always together. That's the key to the whole rewind: a closer Moon means shorter days. |
| r17 | Arka | So the Moon gets a promotion, and Earth pays for it. |

| r20 | Arka | So either the Moon is lying about its age, or the maths is. |

## Honesty notes

- **"Twenty times wider"** uses the 20,000 km estimate; with 25,000 km it's about 15 times. The line says "about", and r50 says "perhaps".
- **The clay tablet (checked 23 Nov):** a published translation says the eclipse began 24 time-degrees (about an hour and a half) after sunrise and that Venus, Mercury and the Normal Stars were visible. r23 now says exactly that, no more.
- **The billion-year stall** is a recent, debated result. Narration says "seems to" and "may have"; no on-screen note should state it as settled.
- **The Moon returning** is textbook physics but needs an undisturbed Earth–Moon system for tens of billions of years; we frame it as "in theory" and say plainly it won't happen.
- **Phobos's end:** crash or ring; we say "or, more likely, break apart", the view of the 2015 studies.
- **Open loop:** r77 teases **E1**. Its script must be at least drafted before M1 is published, or r77–r78 change.
