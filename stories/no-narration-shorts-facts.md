# No-narration Shorts: fact check (26 Nov 2026)

Two Shorts with no narrator: the picture and a live counter carry them, and Arka has one comic line at the end.
Every number below was checked on 26 Nov 2026; calculations are reproducible (US Standard Atmosphere 1976 for air
pressure and temperature).

## A. "Taking the Elevator to Space"

Assumptions (shown in the description, not on screen):
- **Speed: 21 m/s up, 10 m/s down.** The world's fastest lift (Guinness, Hitachi, Guangzhou CTF Finance Centre) was
  tested at 1,260 m/min = 21 m/s going up; its rated descending speed is 600 m/min = 10 m/s.
  Sources: Hitachi press releases 2 Jun 2017 and 27 Sep 2019.
- **One floor = 3.5 m** (typical storey height 3-4.5 m; Hong Kong practice note: typical floor 3.5 m).

| Milestone | Height | Floor | Time going up | Air left (sea level = 100%) | Outside temp | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | 0 | 0 | 0 | 100% | 15 °C | USSA 1976 |
| Burj Khalifa, top | 828 m | 237 | 39 s | 91% | 10 °C | CTBUH / Guinness |
| Everest summit | 8,848.86 m | 2,528 | 7 min 1 s | 31% | -42 °C | Nepal-China survey, Dec 2020 |
| Jets cruise (35,000 ft) | 10.7 km | 3,048 | 8 min 28 s | 24% | -54 °C | typical cruise 31,000-42,000 ft |
| Armstrong limit (exposed saliva and tears boil) | 19.2 km (63,000 ft) | 5,486 | 15 min 14 s | 6.2% | -57 °C | US Standard Atmosphere; Wikipedia "Armstrong limit" |
| Space starts (Kármán line, FAI) | 100 km | 28,571 | 1 h 19 min | almost none | (stop showing) | FAI 100 km boundary |
| ISS | about 419 km (orbit 414-424 km, Nov 2026) | 119,714 | 5 h 33 min | none | (stop showing) | TLE data, Nov 2026 |

Punchline: press "G" at the ISS: going down at 10 m/s takes **11 h 38 min**. Arka: "...I'll take the stairs."
Wording rules: "tears would boil" is right (exposed body fluids, not blood in the veins). Say "about 419 km": the
ISS height changes with every reboost.
Originality note: Neal Agarwal's web page "Space Elevator" (neal.fun) scrolls up through the sky with altitude and
temperature. Ours is a different format and joke (an ordinary office lift, a floor display, Arka), but don't copy its
milestone list or art.

## B. "Walking to the Moon"

Assumptions: walking nonstop at **5 km/h**, **0.75 m per step** (about 1,333 steps per km; common estimates
1,250-1,500), surface to surface.

| Milestone | Distance walked | Time | Steps | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 steps (daily goal) | 7.5 km | 1 h 30 min | 10,000 | step-length estimates |
| ISS height | about 419 km | 3.5 days | 0.56 million | as above |
| Geostationary satellites | 35,786 km | 298 days | 48 million | GEO altitude |
| The Moon (surface to surface) | about 376,300 km (385,000 km centre to centre, minus Earth's and the Moon's radii) | **8.6 years** | **about 500 million** | JPL (385,000 km), NASA/JPL (3.8 cm a year) |

Punchline: in 8.6 years the Moon drifts away about **33 cm** (3.8 cm a year, Lunar Laser Ranging). Arka reaches for
it and misses by a hand: "...It moved."

### Added for Elevator draft 2 (26 Nov 2026)
- **Shooting stars** light up at about 80-120 km (EarthSky, "How high up are meteors when they begin to glow?"); some
  burn out near 100 km, others lower. Shown at 84, 95 and 112 km with the label "Shooting stars burn up here".
- **Northern lights**: green aurora at 100-200 km (oxygen), red above 200 km (NASA Science, "Auroras"; Berkeley ARTEMIS:
  90% of auroral light comes from 100-250 km). Drawn as green curtains from 100 to 200 km with a red fringe above,
  labelled "Northern lights glow up here". Seen from the ISS, auroras lie below the station, as in the ending.
