## Fact check (sources consulted 23 Nov 2026)

| Claim | Line | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Geocorona: a hydrogen cloud where the atmosphere merges into space | g3 | ESA/SOHO 2019 release ("Earth's atmosphere stretches out to the Moon – and beyond"). |
| Reaches 630,000 km, 50 Earth diameters, almost twice the Moon's distance; "the Moon flies through Earth's atmosphere" | g4, g5 | ESA and SOHO hotshot (20 Feb 2019), lead author Igor Baliukin: found by re-examining SWAN observations from more than 20 years earlier ("dusted off"). |
| Apollo 16 photographed the geocorona from the Moon in 1972; astronauts did not know they were embedded in it | g5 | ESA image "Earth's geocorona from the Moon" (UV camera operated by Apollo 16 astronauts); ESA quotes Jean-Loup Bertaux on the astronauts. |
| So thin it would be called a vacuum | g8 | ESA: Baliukin, "On Earth we would call it vacuum". |
| "Space starts 100 km up" | g3 | The Kármán line convention; phrased as "we say", not as a fact. |
| Artemis II, April 2026: farthest humans have travelled, 406,771 km | g1, g6 | NASA release (Artemis II crew back on Earth); AP: 252,756 miles (406,771 km), broke Apollo 13's record; launch 1 Apr, splashdown 10 Apr 2026. 406,771 < 630,000. |
| Hematite (rust) found near the Moon's poles in Chandrayaan-1 M3 data | r3 | NASA/JPL, 2 Sep 2020; Li et al., *Science Advances* 2020. M3 was built by JPL and flew on ISRO's Chandrayaan-1. |
| Earth's oxygen travels along the magnetotail to the Moon | r4, r5 | NASA/JPL 2020 (Kaguya found this in 2007); Axios: "spilling over from Earth's atmosphere, driven by the planet's magnetic field". |
| At full Moon the magnetotail blocks over 99% of the solar wind; solar-wind hydrogen works against rust | r5, r6 | NASA/JPL 2020: hydrogen is a reducer; the tail "blocks over 99 percent of the solar wind" when the Moon is in its full phase. |
| More hematite on the Earth-facing near side | r7 | NASA/JPL 2020. (Small amounts also on the far side; we say "more", not "only".) |

Not claimed: how long the rusting has been going on, or how the water gets there. Li's leading idea is dust impacts freeing water, but it is unconfirmed, so the script leaves it out.
