## Fact check (sources consulted 20 Nov 2026)

| Claim | Line | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Three computers, 69.63 KB of memory combined | m2 | Scientific American ("Voyager Deconstructed"): each Voyager's computer hardware totals 69.63 kilobytes; IEEE Spectrum (Linda Spilker): "a mere 69.63 kilobytes"; IFLScience (Apr 2026) quotes a NASA estimate of 68 KB. Script says "sixty-nine". |
| Less than one phone photo | m1 | Popular Science: "less than a standard JPEG file"; MakeTechEasier: a smartphone photo is several megabytes. |
| 8-track digital tape recorder; 160 bits per second | m4 | Popular Science; Scientific American; NASA image "Voyager's 8-track digital tape recorder". |
| Dial-up ≥ 20,000 bit/s, so >100× faster | m5 | Popular Science (citing Wired): dial-up operates at a minimum of 20,000 bit/s; 20,000 ÷ 160 = 125. |
| Memory chip failure from Nov 2023; fix squeezed into spare memory; usable data again 20 Apr 2024 (~5 months); 24 billion km | m6–m7 | IEEE Spectrum: gibberish from 14 Nov 2023, five months; memory size "the biggest challenge"; 24 billion km. MakeTechEasier/JPL: a single FDS chip failed; usable data resumed 20 Apr 2024. |
| No signal 18 and 19 Oct 2026; returned 20 Oct before any command | l1, l2, l5 | NASA JPL release 2026-078 (29 Oct 2026), reported by MakeTechEasier, Interesting Engineering, The Register. |
| One-way message time ≈ a day | l4 | In Oct 2026 Voyager 1 was ~25.8 billion km away (~23.9 light-hours). |
| Thrusters went offline; backups switched in automatically; cause still under investigation | l6 | Digg/NASA summary: one group of thrusters went offline, backups activated automatically; XenoSpectrum: the thruster switch and the return of contact are both confirmed but not necessarily related. Script does not claim the switch caused the recovery. |
| First object to reach one light-day, 18 Nov 2026 | l8 | NASA/JPL; our V1 Short. |
