# Moon Shorts (demand test for M1): draft 1, 26 Nov 2026

Two picture-first Shorts that test whether "the Moon is leaving" works for us before we finish the long episode M1.
Built from what the winning Moon Shorts do (studied 26 Nov): show the consequence, not the mechanism; one continuous change; the picture fills the frame; the premise stays on screen; 20–30 s; a loop.
Every number comes from fact tables we already checked (`moon-rewind-script.md`, `moon-gone-script.md`).

Voices: narrator Gahanarka R1, Arka E3 (one line each). No brand sting. Not approved; no narration requested.

## Short A: "The Moon Used to Fill Your Sky" (30 chars, about 30 s)

**Banner (on screen throughout):** THE MOON IS SHRINKING
**One shot, never cut:** a sea horizon at night, seen from the shore. The Moon starts enormous and glowing, and shrinks continuously while a counter runs: years ago · distance · length of a day. Arka stands small on the shore the whole time, so the size is felt against a person.

| # | Speaker | Line | Counter shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| a1 | Narrator | Stand here four and a half billion years ago, and the Moon fills your sky. About twenty times wider than tonight. | 4.5 billion years ago · about 20,000 km · day: about 5 h |
| a2 | Narrator | A whole day lasts about five hours. | (same; Sun races across) |
| a3 | Narrator | Then it starts to leave. A little farther every year. A little smaller. And the days stretch. | 2.46 billion years ago · 321,800 km · 16.9 h, then 380 million years ago · day 21.9 h |
| a4 | Narrator | Tonight, a pea at arm's length covers it. And it's still going. | today · 384,400 km · 24 h · +3.8 cm a year |
| a5 | Narrator | In about six hundred million years, it will be too small to cover the Sun. | +600 million years |
| a6 | Arka | Go back. I liked it bigger. | (snaps back to a1's frame: loop) |

About 80 words, about 470 characters.

## Short B: "The Last Total Solar Eclipse" (28 chars, about 26 s)

**Banner:** TOTAL ECLIPSES ARE RUNNING OUT
**One shot, never cut:** a total solar eclipse fills the frame: black disc, white corona. A year counter runs forward; the black disc slowly shrinks until a thin ring of sunlight appears and grows.

| # | Speaker | Line | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| b1 | Narrator | One day, this will happen for the last time. | Totality, corona, silence |
| b2 | Narrator | It only works by luck. The Sun is about four hundred times wider than the Moon, and about four hundred times farther away. A perfect fit. | "400× wider · 400× farther" as two small notes |
| b3 | Narrator | But the Moon is leaving, almost four centimetres a year. Every year, the fit gets worse. | Counter runs; disc shrinks |
| b4 | Narrator | In about six hundred million years, if it keeps that pace, the Moon will be too small. A ring of fire is all that's left. | Ring appears and brightens |
| b5 | Arka | So we got the good seats. By accident. | Arka in eclipse glasses; cut back to totality: loop |

About 75 words, about 430 characters.

## Fact check (all from earlier, sourced tables)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Young Moon about 20,000 km away, about 10° across, roughly 20× wider (15× if 25,000 km) | Scientific American (Plait), see moon-rewind r1–r2, r50; script says "about" |
| Day about 5 hours at the start | Live Science, moon-rewind r52 |
| 2.46 billion years ago: 321,800 km, 16.9 h | Lantink et al., PNAS 2022 |
| 380 million years ago: about 400 days a year, 21.9 h day | Wells 1963 (Nature); calculated |
| 3.8 cm a year; a pea at arm's length covers the Moon | Eos; calculated (moon-rewind r1) |
| Sun about 400× wider and 400× farther | moon-gone m32 (Ep 4) |
| Last total solar eclipse in about 600 million years, if today's rate holds | NASA's Noah Petro, moon-gone m66; both scripts keep the "if" or "about" |

Honesty notes: the counter interpolates smoothly between sourced points, so it carries a small "illustration between measured points" note. Annular (ring) eclipses already happen today; b4 says the ring is "all that's left", which stays true.
